NATURAL MOVEMENT TARGET — DESIGN CONTRACT, NOT A COMPLETED ANIMATION

Purl is the coral player with one curled tuft. Loop is the teal rival with two loops. Preserve their soft round yarn proportions. Their speed should feel like a normal casual running race: lively, with time to react. Calibrate world meters/second, camera distance and stride length together; a hardcoded speed without a known character scale is not a quality guarantee.

Locomotion states: idle, accelerate/run, controlled brake, jump, land, stumble/snared, recover, pull-interaction, finish. Idle has subtle breathing, not repeated two-foot hops. A grounded run has alternating stance and swing with push-off, opposite arm movement, gentle forward lean and limited body bob. Feet may leave the ground during a real run's flight phase. Align every stance foot with the actual projected ground plane. Drive gait phase by distance traveled, not wall-clock time while stationary. Stop the gait when movement stops. A jump uses a separate arc/animation and suppresses run-foot planting until landing.

The current 2D run-left/run-right filenames describe alternate foot poses; do not assume they are left/right-facing turn views. One mirrored rear sprite cannot provide every turn viewpoint. Either build a complete directional set with consistent scale and foot anchors or use a rigged 3D model with grounded animation. The included images are identity/material/pose references, not a finished skeletal run cycle. Review existing alpha edges before final use.

Grounding: position = sampled road center + right-vector * lateral offset + road-up * jump height. The character root/foot anchor is defined in world space. Width bounds include body clearance and railing clearance. Inspect actual visible surface boundaries in debug, not only a route line. Account for the inclined road normal when placing soles/shadows. Respect depth ordering; a foreground pillar must hide the player when it lies between camera and player. No feet on a vertical carpet side, unsupported gap or transition divider.

Steering: a swipe selects a smooth lateral target rather than teleporting. At a junction, lock the pending branch only in a clear decision window; blend position/heading onto the chosen physical route. Keep main and branch progress in comparable race-distance coordinates. Apply the same physical road/collision rules to Loop. Camera follows actual world position, not scene-page progress.

Chase camera: slightly above and behind, aim a short distance ahead along the route. Smooth position and heading with time-based damping, retain enough look-ahead for a bend, avoid clipping through towers. Keep Purl readable near lower-middle and the next action visible. Maintain world continuity when track material changes. No fade, wipe, page slide or scene reset between areas. Do not hold both racers visible by teleporting Loop; use suitable shared/rejoining route design and a small honest lead indicator if needed.

Input: default auto-run; left/right swipe steers or queues a branch; up swipe jumps; double-tap toggles player running/braking. A double-tap is not Pause. UI buttons do not count as gameplay gestures. Multi-touch/cancel must not produce stale actions. Provide button alternatives. Android back/background lifecycle pause is handled by the app host; do not resume the simulation behind a modal or system screen.

The route sampler and input module are reusable implementation examples. They do not provide physics, foot IK, root-motion animation, full opponent AI, Android lifecycle integration or production performance by themselves.
